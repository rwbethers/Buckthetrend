Mistakes 01 – 03
01
Mistake One
You're Invoicing Late — and Waiting Even Longer to Follow Up

Every day an invoice sits unsent is a day you're giving your client an interest-free loan. Most founders batch invoicing at the end of the month — which means work done on the 1st doesn't get paid until the 60th. Multiply that by your entire book of business and you have a structural cash delay baked into your model.

The Fix
Invoice the moment work is delivered. Set up automated payment reminders at 7, 14, and 21 days past due. Offer ACH to eliminate check delays. If you're on Net 30, negotiate Net 15 with your top accounts.
02
Mistake Two
You're Confusing Profit With Cash Flow

Your P&L says you made $80,000 last month. Your bank account says $12,000. Both can be true — and this disconnect is one of the most common sources of founder stress. Profit is an accounting concept. Cash is what you can actually spend. Inventory, timing differences, loan repayments, and prepaid expenses all create gaps between the two.

The Fix
Run a cash flow statement every month — not just the P&L. Your income statement tells you if you're profitable. Your cash flow statement tells you if you can survive. You need both.
03
Mistake Three
You Have No 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast

Operating without a forward-looking cash forecast is like driving at night with your headlights off. You might make it — but you won't see the problem until you're already in it. A 13-week rolling forecast shows you exactly when cash will be tight, weeks before it happens, so you can act rather than react.

The Fix
Build a simple 13-week forecast in Excel or your accounting software. Map every expected inflow and outflow week-by-week. Review it every Monday. This single habit changes how you run your business.

Mistakes 04 – 06
04
Mistake Four
Your Payroll Timing Is Destroying Your Cash Position

Payroll is typically the largest fixed outflow in any business. When it's not aligned with your revenue collection cycle, it creates predictable cash crunches — often at the worst possible time. Many founders don't realize their payroll dates are working against them until cash gets tight two months in a row at the same point in the cycle.

The Fix
Map your payroll dates against your average receivables collection window. If there's a consistent gap, either adjust payroll timing, accelerate collections, or establish a line of credit as a bridge — before you need it.
05
Mistake Five
You Applied for Credit When You Already Needed It

Banks lend to businesses that don't need money. The moment your financials show stress — declining revenue, tight cash, missed payments — your borrowing options shrink and your rates go up. Most founders wait until they're desperate, which is exactly the wrong time to call their banker.

The Fix
Establish a business line of credit when your financials are clean and strong. Draw nothing. Pay it back immediately if you use it. Having access to capital when you don't need it is one of the best financial decisions a business owner can make.
06
Mistake Six
You Don't Know Your Cash Conversion Cycle

Your Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) measures how long it takes to turn a dollar spent on operations into a dollar collected from a customer. A long CCC means your money is tied up in the business — in inventory, work-in-progress, or unpaid invoices. Most owners have never calculated theirs.

The Fix
Calculate: Days Sales Outstanding + Days Inventory Outstanding – Days Payable Outstanding. Shorten it by collecting faster, turning inventory quicker, and extending vendor payment terms. Every day you shorten your CCC frees up real cash.

Mistakes 07 – 10
07
Mistake Seven
Tax Surprises Are Draining Your Operating Cash

A $60,000 tax bill in April is devastating if you haven't been setting money aside. Many founders don't have accurate quarterly tax projections — so they either pay penalties for underpayment or scramble to find cash that should have been reserved months ago. Either way, it's avoidable.

The Fix
Work with your CPA to project quarterly tax obligations and set aside cash in a dedicated tax reserve account monthly — not quarterly. Treat it like a bill, not an afterthought. No surprises in April.
08
Mistake Eight
You're Overpaying on Vendor Terms You Never Renegotiated

The terms you signed when you were a small client are rarely the best terms you can get once you've grown. Vendors want to keep your business. Most founders never go back to renegotiate — they just keep paying Net 15 on a $400,000 annual account that could easily support Net 45 or Net 60.

The Fix
Audit your top 10 vendor relationships annually. Request extended payment terms from your largest suppliers. Even moving Net 15 to Net 30 across $500K in payables creates meaningful cash flow breathing room.
09
Mistake Nine
Your HR Costs Are Unmanaged and Growing Quietly

Benefits, workers' comp, overtime, turnover costs, and compliance penalties rarely show up as a single line item — they're scattered across payroll, insurance, and legal fees. Without a dedicated HR function reviewing these, they compound quietly for years. One wrongful termination claim or benefits compliance error can cost more than a year of HR oversight.

The Fix
Get a fractional HR team to audit your people costs, ensure compliance, and optimize your benefits structure. The cost of prevention is a fraction of the cost of a single HR incident.
